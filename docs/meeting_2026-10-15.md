# Meeting with Hugo, 15 October 2026 — splits, test boxes and the 2024 data

Prepared 5 October 2026. Nothing here is decided: it is a proposal to discuss with Hugo, then to
write into CHARTER.md once agreed. Counts come from notebooks 01 and 02 (5 October 2026).

## 1. What the data look like, in short

- 2526: 15 boxes of 30 fruit, box letter = orchard ID. 433 scanned. Grades depend strongly on the
  orchard: box K has 27 of 30 fruit without browning, box O has 15 with browning 3. Browning 2–3
  without any cavity (the hard case we care most about): 73 fruit, 0 to 11 per box.
- 2425: defect is fully confounded with storage group. All 300 fruit after suboptimal storage have
  browning; none of the 300 at harvest or the 60 after optimal storage have browning or cavity.
- Fruit volume in voxels differs strongly between 2526 orchards (box medians 120,805 in K to
  228,988 in I). Every fruit is rescaled to the same length, so this measures how slender the
  fruit are. K is also the healthiest box: shape is a possible shortcut.
- Rot (2425): 16 fruit with rot = 1, 11 of them with browning 3 and cavity 3. Of the 175 fruit
  with browning 3 and cavity 3, only 11 are rotten. So "rotten fruit were given the highest grades"
  holds for most rotten fruit, but most highest-grade fruit are not rotten.

## 2. Proposed rule for the final test boxes

1. **Written before it is applied.** The rule below is committed to git with a date before any box
   is drawn.
2. **Restricted randomisation.** List all 455 ways to choose 3 of the 15 boxes. Keep the sets
   whose counts, on the 433 scanned fruit, are close to the whole of 2526 on: browning 2–3 without
   cavity, browning 3, any cavity, browning 3 and cavity 3. Proposed tolerance: each count within
   ±30 % of its expected value for 3 boxes (to be fixed before drawing). Draw one of the
   remaining sets with a fixed seed and record the seed. Only label counts are used, never CT
   features.
3. **Sealed.** The fruit identifiers go in `derived/test_boxes.csv`; one function removes them by
   default in every notebook. Test fruit are encoded (the encoder sees no labels) but their labels
   appear in no plot or table. This needs one change to rule 2 of CHARTER.md ("exploration and
   plots may use all fruit") — add "except the test boxes".
4. **Opened once.** After the whole pipeline is frozen (features, model, settings, exclusions,
   threshold), one run on the test boxes, reported whatever it shows, recorded in a `run.json`
   marked "final test".
5. **Known limit.** About 85 fruit and about 15 cases of browning without cavity: the test confirms
   the cross-validation estimate on untouched orchards, it does not give a precise number. A
   bootstrap over 3 boxes is not possible; the test interval resamples fruit and says so.

Cross-validation on the remaining 12 boxes: nested and grouped at both levels (outer folds to
estimate performance, inner folds to choose settings such as the number of PLS components), 4
outer folds of 3 boxes chosen by the same balanced search, repeated over a few partitions to show
how much the result depends on which boxes share a fold. Beside the pooled score, a within-box
score (area under the curve per box, averaged over boxes with enough cases), so a model that only
separates healthy orchards from unhealthy ones is visible.

## 3. Questions for Hugo on splits

1. How would you set the test boxes? Do you agree with holding out 3 orchards, or would you rather
   rely on cross-validation alone given 15 orchards?
2. Your classifier (`train_clf_3d.py`) puts fruit of every box in train, validation and test. Was

3. Within one 2526 box: same orchard, same storage, same scan session? Were the 15 orchards stored

4. Are 2024 and 2526 orchards with the same letter different orchards?
5. Which 2526 fruit were rotten? Until we know, we flag all 24 with browning 3 and cavity 3, which
   probably removes many real severe cases (see the 2425 numbers above).
6. Can you share the list of the 2024 fruit in your VAE test split (seed 42, about 66 fruit)? Those
   are 2024 fruit the encoder has not seen.

## 4. Can the 2024–2025 data still be used?

Yes, but not as a healthy-versus-defective test, because there defect equals storage group. Uses
that stay sound:

1. **Severity within the stored fruit.** Among the 300 fruit after suboptimal storage, browning
   ranges from 1 to 3. An ordinal model within that group does not compare storage groups. The
   encoder saw about 80 % of these fruit; state it.
2. **The reverse cross-season check** already in rule 2 of CHARTER.md (fit on 2526, evaluate on
   2024), restricted to the stored fruit or reported per storage group.
3. **The gate test.** Cut-open photographs exist only for 2024–2025, so any check of where the
   browning is (whether the latent changes at the lesion) can only be done there.
4. **Hugo's VAE test fruit** (question 6), if he can share the list: 2024 fruit that are both
   unseen and graded.
5. **Healthy reference.** The 360 fruit at harvest and after optimal storage show what normal
   variation in the latent looks like (size, shape, orchard) without any defect.

Not sound: training a detector on all 660 fruit and reporting its accuracy as browning detection;
it would detect storage group.

## 5. The title

"Latent space is all you need" reads as a claim; the charter asks a question ("how much of the
information … and under what conditions does it stop being enough?"). The expected answer from the
numbers in CLAUDE.md is "enough for grade 3, not for grade 1", which makes the title partly false.
Worth asking Hugo whether the title is meant as the hypothesis being tested (then a question mark
or a subtitle helps) or as the expected conclusion.
