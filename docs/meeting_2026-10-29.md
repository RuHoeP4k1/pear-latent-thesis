### D. Definitions to be able to state in the methods (and to tell Hugo)

Ruben asked on 23 October to keep this text for the methods section and the meeting. How each
colouring (descriptor) is measured. All are computed by `pearlatent.descriptors` on the 128³
volumes, in grid voxels, so they are relative: each fruit was rescaled to 128 voxels in length.

- **Volume:** threshold at 0.1 of the min-max-scaled intensity, keep the largest connected
  part, fill enclosed holes, count voxels.
- **Body:** the same, minus the stalk, using the same body mask as the latent features.
- **Stalk length:** slices of fruit beyond the end of the body, where a body slice has at least
  4 latent cells. **Stalk present:** stalk length of at least 10 voxels.
- **Flat face:** RANSAC (random sample consensus) plane search over the surface voxels: a plane
  through 3 random surface voxels, the surface voxels within 1 voxel of it counted, 3,000
  planes, seed 369; the largest connected patch on the best plane, divided by all surface
  voxels. A screen for cut volumes only; the eye decides.

Not size in millimetres: that needs the original length of every fruit (open question 18).

**1. Which fruit (notebook 06 section 1; 1 min).** 478 fruit decoded: the 346 open 2526
fruit, his 66 validation fruit and a sample of 66 of his 528 training fruit (seed 369, never
pooled with the others). After the 35 exclusions: 443, of which 315 are 2526. Every map and
spectrum uses the 315 2526 fruit.

**2. Reconstruction errors (sections 2 and 3): skip unless he asks.** If he asks: computed
during the encoding run from the full 3D reconstruction `decode(mu)`, which is then
discarded; core MAE by grade 0 to 3 is 0.0120, 0.0147, 0.0198, 0.0213; not directly
comparable with his scores (`decode(mu)` against `vae(x)`, no augmentation, `best.pth`).

**3. Contrast retention (section 4; 3 min).**
- How: per fruit the mean CT and the mean reconstruction over the core voxels of the two
  central long sections; per grade the difference from grade 0 in both, and their ratio.
  1 = the darkening is fully kept, 0 = the reconstructed core looks healthy. Interval:
  bootstrap over the 12 open boxes (2,000 draws, seed 369).
- The core: body voxels within one third of the widest radius of the axis, widest radius =
  sqrt(largest slice area / pi). Fixed on 13 October, before any reconstruction was seen.
- Caveats: two long sections only, not the full 3D core (question 35); grade 2 is wide
  because the CT difference itself is tiny; cavity only has 1 and 2 fruit, no claim.
- If he asks why the maps use a different radius: the core definition was fixed before the
  results and `mae_core` needs the full reconstructions, which are not saved (about 5.5
  hours to decode the 2526 fruit again). On the map radius the core edge lies at 0.32
  (median over the 346 open 2526 fruit; 5 % to 95 %: 0.30 to 0.34; measured 27 October).

**4. Height-radius maps, CT and reconstruction (section 5, view "CT and reconstruction";
4 min).**
- How, in one breath: every voxel of the two long sections gets a height (0 at the calyx end
  of the body, 1 at the stalk end, stalk removed) and a radius (distance to the stem-calyx
  axis divided by the distance of the outermost body voxel: 0 axis, 1 skin). Both sides of
  the axis are folded onto one. Per fruit the mean in each of 32 × 24 bins, then the mean
  over the fruit of a group, so every fruit counts once. A bin only where at least half of
  the group's fruit reach it.
- Why this coordinate: preprocessing aligned the stem-calyx axis and rescaled every fruit to
  128 voxels in length, so height is comparable between fruit; rotation about the axis is
  not, and averaging over the angle removes it.
- Rows: CT, CT minus grade 0, reconstruction minus grade 0, residual. Where rows 2 and 3
  look alike, the reconstruction keeps the darkening.
- Caveats: two sections per fruit; each group is a different set of fruit, and the groups
  differ in size, stalk and box (step 7).

**5. Latent maps (section 5, the two "latent" views; 3 min).**
- How: the same height and radius for every body cell of the 32³ latent `mu` (all cells,
  so the full rotation), 20 × 8 bins of about one cell each. Two ways to normalise the radius:
  one per fruit (outermost cell of the fruit; keeps the shape) or one per slice (outermost
  cell of that slice; radius 1 = skin at every height). No value is interpolated in any map:
  each cell only gets a coordinate.
- How to read it (a property of his encoder, not of the method): a latent value has no unit
  and no sign meaning (KL weight 1e-6, a compression latent): lower is not darker. One latent cell is computed from a block of about 31 voxels
  (about 2 cm) around it, roughly half the fruit radius to each side, and InstanceNorm adds
  information from the whole fruit. So the latent map says roughly where (about ±1 cm), the
  CT map says where.

**6. Examples (sections 6 and 7; 2 min).** One fruit per grade, the one with the core MAE
closest to its group's median (not chosen by eye). Scroll in the 3D viewer: the fine texture
is smoothed.

**7. Latent structure (notebook 07 sections 1 and 2; 3 min).** Item 2 of Part 2: PC1 follows
body size in the grid and stalk length; grade is mixed everywhere. Then the size and stalk
difference between the map groups (update of 27 October) and question 38.

**8. Spectra (notebook 07 section 3; 4 min).**
- How: the 20 × 8 bins of a fruit in one row (bin = 8 × height bin + radius bin), mean
  spectrum per group with the 25 % to 75 % band (spread between fruit, not an interval).
  Show the comparison figure of the two radius methods first, then the drag box both ways.
- Corrections: minus the grade 0 mean; divided by the grade 0 standard deviation; minus the

- What to say: for looking only, nothing fitted or tested; bins picked by eye are not a
